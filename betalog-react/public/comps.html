For climbing centres ↑ top

A comp night normally means printing scorecards, handing them out, chasing them back and adding them up. BetaComp puts the scorecard on each climber's phone and does the rest.

Nothing to install

It is a website: betalog.co.uk. Climbers use their own phones. You need a QR code on a poster and that's it. No kit, no tablets at the desk.

Runs on the clock

Set a start and end time. Cards open at the start and lock at the end by themselves, even when no staff phone is awake.

Live leaderboard

Per category and overall, updated as climbers score. You choose whether climbers can see it during the comp or only at the end.

Judging built in

When scoring ends there is a judging window. Open any climber's card and amend a problem, or clear it, with a note. They see why.

Hidden grades

Keep some or all grades secret during the comp. They are revealed to everyone when you publish the results.

Results in a spreadsheet

Export the final results with one tap: every climber, every problem. Ready for prizes, social posts or your records.

Repeatable

Running a monthly league? Copy to a new comp keeps the format, scoring and scoresheet. Just set the new grades.

Climbers keep it

Each climber's card becomes a climbing session in their own training log. Their comp counts toward their grade and level the same evening.

What a centre needs

  • One organiser account. A free BetaLog account (Google, or email and password). The person who sets it up is the organiser.
  • A poster with the QR code. Opening entries gives the comp a join code like CP-K7M2Q and a QR code to print or put on a screen.
  • Climbers with phones. Each climber signs in with a free account to enter, so their card is theirs and nobody else can score it. The card keeps working with no signal and catches up when it has some.

Honest limits, today

  • Climbers score themselves. Just like a paper card. The organiser corrects a card by amending it. There is no separate judge sign-off per problem, and only organisers can amend.
  • No entry fees or payments. Take those however you do now.
  • Categories are a tick box. A climber picks Female, Under 16 or whatever you set. Nothing is checked, same as a paper form.
  • Bouldering first. Rope comps work with the same card, where the zone is a marked hold and the top is the chains. There are no lead falls or height scoring.

How a comp runs ↑ top

Every comp goes through the same five stages. The comp's pages show them as a row of steps with the current one lit, so the organiser and every climber always know where things stand. Two of the moves happen by themselves on the clock. The others are a tap on the organiser's phone.

An example comp night: entries open during the week, scoring runs 18:00–20:00, then a short judging window before the organiser publishes the results.

  1. 1

    Draft Organiser starts it

    The organiser fills in the details, the scoring, the categories and the scoresheet. It is saved on their phone only. Nobody else can see it yet.

  2. 2

    Pending start Tap: Open entries

    OrganiserGets the join code and QR code for the poster. Can still change anything, including the times.
    ClimbersScan the code, tap Enter, pick a name for the board and a category. Their card is ready but locked.
  3. 3

    Running Automatic at the start time

    OrganiserWatches the board fill up. Can move the end time later, or sooner (not before now). No start time set? Tap Start scoring now.
    ClimbersEvery card opens. They tap each go, the zone and the top. The time left is shown at the top of the card.
  4. 4

    Judging Automatic at the end time

    OrganiserChecks the cards. Taps a climber on the board and amends anything wrong, with a note.
    ClimbersCards lock and say Time's up. They can see their card, and any amendment with its note.

    Need more time? Fire alarm, late start? Reopen scoring takes a new end time and puts the comp back to Running.

  5. 5

    Final Tap: Close and publish results

    OrganiserThe results are final. Export them as a spreadsheet. Copy the comp to start next month's.
    ClimbersSee the final board and every hidden grade. Their card is a finished session in their climbing log.

    Final never happens by itself. The organiser always gets the last word.

3

Organising a comp ↑ top

Tap the trophy in BetaLog's top bar, then Organise a competition. You fill in four things. It saves as a draft as you go.

  1. Details. A name, boulder or rope, the date, the start and end times, the venue, and a line of notes for the poster.
  2. Scoring. The maximum goes per problem, what a top is worth on each go, what a zone is worth, and whether every problem counts or only a climber's best few. The defaults work well, and a sentence underneath says the rule back to you in plain English. See Scoring.
  3. Categories. Open alone, or Female · Male · Under 16, whatever the night needs.
  4. Scoresheet. The problems: number, colour, points and grade. A new comp starts with ten (green 10 points, blue 20, red 30, black 50). Generate a sheet by circuit builds any other set in one go.

Then tap Open entries. It checks everything is filled in and gives you the join code and QR code.

Grades, and why every problem has one

The setter grades every problem, even the ones they hide. Hidden grades never reach a climber's phone until the close, so they can't be peeked at. The grades matter because each problem a climber tries becomes a climb at that grade in their own log.

Once scoring has started

The scoring rule, the points and the problem list are fixed, because every card is being scored against them. You can still add a problem, show or hide a grade, and move the end time.

A checklist for the night

  • Set the start and end times, so nobody needs to press anything to begin or finish.
  • Print the QR poster, or put it on a screen by the desk.
  • Enter from a second phone yourself before the night, to see what climbers see.
  • Tell climbers one thing: tap + for every go, then Zone or Top on the go you got it.
  • Leave ten minutes after the end for judging, then Close and publish results.
4

Climbing in a comp ↑ top

  1. Scan the QR code on the poster, or type the code into Join under the trophy in BetaLog.
  2. Tap Enter, give the name you want on the board, and pick a category.
  3. On the night, tap as you climb. + for each go, then Zone or Top on the go you got it. A top on the first go reads Flash. Miscounted? Once it's topped, − and + move the top to the right go and the points follow. Your score updates as you tap.

It lands in your training log

Your card is a climbing session in BetaLog from your first go. Each problem you tried becomes a climb at its grade: a flash, a send with the real number of goes, or an attempt. Your grade pyramid and level include it the same evening, so don't log the session again by hand. Hidden grades fill in the moment the comp closes.

Scoring ↑ top

Each problem has points. A top earns a share of them depending on which go it came on. A zone without a top earns a smaller share. The organiser sets the numbers, and these are the defaults:

Top on go1 (flash)234 or 5Zone only
Share of the points100%80%60%50%25%

A climber can have at most 5 goes on each problem, and the card stops counting there.

Ties, and "best N"

A climber's score is their problems added up. Equal scores are split by more tops, then more zones, then fewer goes. For a big scoresheet, the organiser can count only each climber's best N problems, so nobody has to try all 30 to compete.

6

The board and judging ↑ top

The Board ranks everyone: one board per category, with Overall first. Your own row is pinned at the top. Scores are worked out from the cards, never typed in. Climbers' boards refresh every 30 seconds. The organiser's updates with every go.

For the organiser

  • Show or hide it. Leaderboard visible to entrants hides the board from climbers until the close, for a surprise results reveal. Organisers always see it.
  • Check a card. Tap any climber to see their card problem by problem.
  • Amend a problem. Tap Amend and the problem opens with the climber's own controls: set the goes, the zone and the top to what really happened, or tap Clear (void) to put it back to no goes. Add a note, like Top not held or Flash seen by judge. The climber sees it on their card, their score and the board update, and what it was is kept on record. You can amend until you publish the results.
  • Export. Export results on Manage gives a spreadsheet of every climber and every problem.
7

Who sees what ↑ top

  • The organiser sees each climber's board name, category and card.
  • Other climbers see board names, categories, scores, tops, zones and goes on the leaderboard: during the comp if the organiser leaves it visible, and always once it is final.
  • Nothing else in a climber's BetaLog is visible to anyone through a comp. Not their other sessions, not their grades, not their email.

8

Questions ↑ top

Do climbers have to download an app?

No. It is a website, so the QR code opens it in the phone's browser. They sign in with Google or an email address. They can add it to their home screen if they like, but they don't have to.

What if the wall has no signal?

The card keeps every tap on the phone and sends it when there is signal again. Opening the app, or coming back to it, re-sends anything waiting.

What does it cost?

BetaLog is free, for climbers and for organisers.

Can people cheat?

About as much as with a paper card. Climbers enter their own scores. What BetaComp adds is visibility: the organiser can open any card at any time, the board shows everyone's tops and goes, and the organiser can amend any problem with a note.

We run a comp every month. Do we start from scratch?

No. Copy to a new comp starts a new draft with the same format, scoring, categories and scoresheet, dated today. The grades are left blank for the new set.

Can we change things once it has started?

Before the start, anything. While it's running you can move the end time, add problems and show or hide grades, but not change the points or scoring. In judging you can reopen scoring with a new end time.

Rope comps?

Yes. Choose Rope as the type: the same card, with French grades. The zone is a marked hold and the top is the chains. Climbs land in each climber's log as top rope.
