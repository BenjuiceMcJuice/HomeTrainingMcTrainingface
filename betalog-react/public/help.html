What BetaLog is ↑ top

BetaLog is a training log for climbers. You write down what you did — a climbing session, a gym workout, a hangboard routine, a run or a swim, a weigh-in, a drink — and it reads what that adds up to: the grade you own, whether you are ready for the next one, how the week went against the plan you made for it.

It is free. It runs in your browser and installs on your phone like an app. It needs an account, so your log is the same on every device you sign in on, and so your friends can see how you are getting on.

What it is not. BetaLog describes your log. It does not tell you what to climb, and a grade it has nothing to say about is not a grade you cannot do — only one you have not logged enough of yet.

Getting started ↑ top

Put it on your phone

There is no app store listing. You install it from the browser, and it then opens full-screen from its own icon like any other app.

iPhoneOpen betalog.co.uk in Safari. Tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen. AndroidOpen it in Chrome. Tap the menu (three dots), then Install app or Add to Home screen.

Sign in

The app does not work without an account. You can sign in with Google, or with an email address and a password. To make an email account, tap Use email instead, then Don't have an account? Sign up. Passwords need at least six characters. Your sign-in is remembered on the device.

  • Google sign-in does not work inside the installed app on iPhone. Open betalog.co.uk in Safari, sign in with Google there, and the home-screen app is signed in too. Or use an email account, which works everywhere.
  • There is no forgotten-password link yet. If you are locked out of an email account, ask through Send feedback and it will be sorted by hand.

The first minute

On first sign-in the Settings sheet opens by itself and asks for your name. That is the name your friends will see. The built-in exercises and three hangboard routines are already there, so the Log tab is ready to use straight away.

Offline. Your log is kept on the phone, so once installed and signed in the app opens and logs without a connection, and syncs when it has one. Signing in, sync, the Coach and Friends need to be online.

Getting around ↑ top

Five tabs along the bottom on a phone, along the top on a computer.

DashboardWhat your log adds up to: your grade, your week, your trends. Read here, change things elsewhere. LogWhere you record a session, a weigh-in or a drink. HistoryEverything you have logged, newest first. Tap a session to edit or delete it. PlanYour goals, your weekly schedule and reminders, your routines and your exercise library. CoachAn AI reading of your last 30 days, in one of four voices. Needs a free key.

Three buttons sit in the top bar: Help (the blue chip, which opens this guide and the feedback form), Friends (the people icon) and Settings (the cog).

4

Log ↑ top

A switcher at the top of the Log tab picks what you are logging: Train · Climb · Hang · Cardio · Health. Every session takes an effort rating and a date, and the date can be in the past, so a session you forgot can be logged later. Saving shows Session saved.

Climb

Pick a discipline — Boulder, Lead or Top Rope. Boulders are graded on the V-scale, V0 to V17; ropes in French grades, 4 to 9c. The grades appear as a row of chips coloured by level. Tap a grade, then tap one of four outcomes, and that adds one climb to the session:

FlashSent first go, no falls. SendClimbed clean, top to bottom, on any attempt. AttemptHad a go, fell. ProjectWorked on it, did not send. Same as an attempt for the maths.

Log each climb as you do it, or the whole session afterwards; the list is headed This session and any row can be removed. You can mix disciplines in one session. Then rate the session — Session feel, 1 Easy to 5 Max — add a note if you like, and save.

Where you climbed is optional but worth typing once: every venue you have ever written becomes a tap. The map-pin beside the field asks your phone where you are and offers the venues you have saved within 300 m, nearest first; if none is that close it offers your five most recent instead. Your location is used only to pick from your own list — there is no map and no venue lookup.

Train

A gym session. Choose an exercise from the library, or a routine you built in Plan, and a sheet opens with the sets filled in from the exercise's defaults or the routine's targets. Change sets, reps (or seconds, for timed exercises) and weight as you go. The weight list runs from assisted (a negative number, for a band or a pulley) through BW for bodyweight to added weight in kilos.

In a routine, tick each exercise off as you finish it. If you tick some and not others, the ones you skipped are saved as skipped and the History card shows 3/5. Tick none and the whole routine counts as done.

Hang

The hangboard timer. Free Hang sets up one grip: fingers, grip type, edge size, hang and rest seconds, sets, reps, weight. Routines runs a routine you built in Plan or one of the three built in. Tap Go or Start and the timer goes full screen with a 15-second Get ready, then Hang, Rest, Set rest and Next grip phases, each in its own colour with a big countdown and a picture of the grip.

  • Sounds. A tick on each of the last three seconds of every phase, a high beep when a hang starts, a low one when a rest starts, a double low beep before a new set or grip, three rising notes when you are done. They are made by the phone, so nothing to download. If your headphones hear them late, raise Beep timing in Settings.
  • The screen stays awake while the timer runs, where the phone allows it.
  • Pause, Skip and End are on screen. When it finishes you rate the session and save. The session is saved as the routine was planned; if you pressed End early, the log still shows the full routine.

Cardio

Six buttons: Swim · Walk · Run · Cycle · Sport · Other. Sport opens a list of 32 sports, from badminton to yoga; Other asks you to name the activity. Duration and distance are both optional — log a run you did not time, or a walk you did not measure. Swims are logged in lengths, with the stroke and the pool length, and the sheet converts to metres for you. Then an effort rating, a note, a date, save.

Calories are estimated when you save, from your weight and whatever you gave it: distance, pace, or just effort and time. That needs a weigh-in on the Health tab dated on or before the session; without one there is no estimate. The figure appears on the History card and in the session's details, as a range. If your distance and time work out faster than a walk, run, ride or swim goes, an amber line asks you to check them; it never stops you saving.

Health

Two cards. Weight takes a number in kilos; one entry a day, and a second weigh-in the same day replaces the first. Alcohol takes drinks: pick a type (beer or cider, wine, spirit, other), and the millilitres and strength are filled in from a typical serving — a pint at 4.5%, 175 ml of wine at 13% — for you to correct. It shows the UK units live, green up to two, amber above, red above six, and keeps a running total for the day.

Changing or deleting a session

Go to History, tap the session, and use Edit or Delete at the bottom of its details. Delete asks for a second tap. Edit reopens the same sheet you logged it in, filled in, so a climb session can have climbs added or removed, a gym session its sets changed, a hangboard session its grips changed. A cardio session's activity type cannot be changed; delete and relog instead. Weigh-ins and drinks work the same way from their own rows in the feed.

Your grade and your goals ↑ top

This is the part of the app that thinks, so it is worth a minute. The full reasoning, with the sources, is in the explainer; this is the short version.

Three readings, one window

Everything about your grade is read over the last 180 days, so it describes the climber you are now. For each discipline:

BaseThe hardest grade you have sent at least 8 times in the window. The grade you own. This is your level, your Currently, and what your friends see. BestThe hardest single send in the window. FlashThe hardest grade you have sent first go.

Base is deliberately lower than Best. Sending one 6c says you got up a 6c; sending eight says you are a 6c climber. No base yet means the log does not hold eight sends at any one grade — it is the app declining to guess, not a judgement.

Levels

A level is a word for a band of grades, read from your base and shown as a coloured badge. Every grade in the app is coloured by its band.

LevelV-scaleFrench
BeginnerV0 – V14 – 5+
IntermediateV2 – V36a – 6b+
AdvancedV4 – V56c – 7a+
ExpertV6 – V77b – 7c+
EliteV8 – V98a – 8a+
ProV10 – V11—
World ClassV12 +8b +

The pyramid

To be ready for a grade you want a broad base of sends underneath it: 8 at four grades below, 4 at three below, 2 at two below, 1 at the grade beneath. That 8 · 4 · 2 · 1 shape is the oldest tool in climbing coaching, and the chart on your climbing widget and your goal card is it, filled from your log. Harder sends count downwards, a pyramid is only as strong as its thinnest row, and under the chart the app tells you the smallest thing that would help: Log 3 more 6a+s to fill the pyramid.

Goals

A grade goal is a grade, a date, and one of two kinds:

Send 6cDone on one send or flash at 6c or harder. The first 6c is the one you remember. Own 6cDone when your base reaches 6c — eight sends. The slower, bigger goal.

Goals tick themselves off: log the send and the goal is achieved, dated to the send, with a row in History that day. A send from before the goal was set counts too, so the app will not let you set a goal you have already met — it tells you when you did it and offers the Own goal instead. You can also set a bodyweight goal and a distance goal for a run, swim or cycle. One goal of each type at a time; edit the one you have rather than stacking another.

The dots and the date

Five dots on every goal answer one question: at your current rate, will you make the date? Five green means time to spare; one red means the date has gone or is far out of reach. The words beside them describe the pyramid, not the date — Pyramid forming with five green dots means it is thin but a year is plenty. Underneath, a forecast says when you would be ready at your current rate, and what the same sum gives if you climbed weekly. It is arithmetic on your log, split into its parts so you can argue with each; it is not a percentage chance, because that would be invented.

Dashboard ↑ top

The Dashboard reads your log back to you. Nothing on it is edited here; tap through to Plan or Log to change things.

At the top, three numbers: your weekly streak (consecutive weeks with at least one session), sessions this week, and your total. Below that, if you have a schedule, a line saying what is Due today — tap a routine's chip and it opens ready to log — or what is next.

The widgets

Each widget is a card. Tap its header to fold or unfold it; a folded card keeps its headline so the page stays short. Tap Edit layout at the bottom to drag them into your order and to switch any of them off. A widget with nothing to show stays out of the way on its own.

WidgetWhat it shows
Training loadYour last 7 days against the month before, as sessions × effort. Easing off, Sweet spot, Pushing hard.
ShameometerThe week so far, scored out of 100. Explained below.
Gym statsGym sessions and sets in the window, and which muscle group is getting the most.
Cardio statsSessions, minutes and distance by activity, estimated calories, and progress on any run, swim or cycle goal.
Boulder level · Rope levelYour level badge and Base · Best · Flash, the pyramid for your next grade or your goal, the forecast, and a chart of your sends by grade.
Alcohol over time & streakYour alcohol-free streak and its next milestone, and units per week against the UK guideline of 14.
Coach tipOne sentence from your chosen coach, once a day. Only if you have a Groq key.
Weight & BMILatest weight, BMI, the trend, and your weight goal if you have one.
Activity calendarA month grid coloured by what you did each day. Tap a day to see it. Scheduled days you missed are shaded.

Most widgets have chips for the window they read — 30d · 90d · 12m; the level cards offer 90d · All time for the send chart. Your choice is kept per widget and follows you between devices.

The Shameometer

A score for the current week, Monday to Sunday, out of 100. Training is worth up to 75: a climbing or hangboard session scores 3, cardio 2, a walk 1, a gym session 1 to 3 by how many exercises it had, with at most 4 points a day and a target of 9 for the week. Schedule is worth up to 25: the share of your scheduled days on which you logged what was due. With nothing scheduled, training alone can reach 100. A dry week adds 10; units over the guideline take points off. The targets scale to how far through the week you are, so Tuesday is not scored against a full week.

When a week ends it is sealed the next time you open the app, and never rescored, so the ten bars of past weeks under the dial are a record, not an estimate. The band names are blunt on purpose.

History ↑ top

One feed, newest first, grouped by day. Each day's card lists its sessions, then any weigh-in, then any drinks. A session shows its type, its name, its effort, and a one-line summary: for a climb, how many climbs and the hardest send with its level; for a gym session, the exercises; for cardio, the distance, time and calories.

Tap a session for the full details, and for Edit and Delete. Tap a weigh-in or a drink to edit or delete it.

When a goal is achieved a green row appears here — Goal achieved · Send 6a, with what did it — on the day it happened, not the day the app noticed. This is also the only place an achieved goal can be removed.

8

Plan ↑ top

Four tabs, and it opens on Goals: a schedule, a routine and an exercise all exist to move a goal.

Goals

+ Add opens the goal sheet. Pick a type — Boulder, Rope, Run, Swim, Cycle or Weight — a target and a date. For a grade you also pick the kind, Send or Own, and the grade chips are coloured by how built your pyramid is for each: green for ready, amber for part-built. The sheet shows the pyramid and the forecast live as you choose, and refuses a goal the log has already met. A weight goal shows the rate it implies and will not save one that is unhealthy.

The goal card then carries everything from chapter 5: your current base, the dots, the pyramid, what to log next, and the forecast. When a goal is achieved the card says Complete! and offers Set your next goal →, one grade up.

Schedule

Up to three routines, each with the days of the week it is due and up to four reminder times. Pick a routine, tap its days, add a time or leave it as No reminder. The Dashboard reads this for Due today, the Shameometer for its schedule points, and the calendar for its shaded missed days.

Reminders can reach your phone two ways, and both are off until you turn them on:

CalendarTap Set up calendar reminders and the app publishes your schedule as a calendar feed, then Subscribe on this device. Alerts come from your Calendar app, so they arrive even if you have not opened BetaLog in weeks. On iPhone one setting has to change — the card walks you through it. The feed link is private: anyone with it can see your routine names and times, and revoking it stops every device at once. NotificationsTap Turn on notifications and your phone asks once. A notification arrives at each reminder time, in your own timezone, naming the routine. On iPhone this is only offered once BetaLog is on your Home Screen. Turn it on separately on each device you want notified.

Routines

Two lists. Training routines are gym sessions: a name, an ordered list of exercises, and for each the sets, reps or seconds, rest and weight you intend. Hanging routines are for the hangboard: a list of grips, each with fingers, grip type, edge, hang and rest seconds, sets, reps and weight. Three hangboard routines are built in — Max Hangs, 7:3 Repeaters and Sub-Max Repeaters — and can be edited but not deleted. Star a routine to keep it at the top.

Exercises

Your exercise library: 96 built in, across arms, legs, mobility, back, shoulders, core, chest and cardio, plus any you add with + Add Exercise. Tap one to see or edit its defaults — sets, reps or duration, rest and weight — and those defaults are what the log sheet fills in. Weight follows the same rule everywhere: 0 is bodyweight, positive is added, negative is assisted. Each exercise has a How to link that searches YouTube. Built-in exercises can be reset to their defaults; your own can be deleted.

Coach ↑ top

The Coach reads your last 30 days and writes a structured review: a summary, flags for recovery, projecting and plateau, and two to four focus areas. It is not a chat. You tell it what you are working towards in one line, choose a voice, and tap Analyse my training.

Jonas RidgeElite coach — calm, precise, data-driven. CrankMaster ChadBoulder bro — hype, stoke, surprisingly smart. Dr Marina SorelSports physiologist — dry, precise, faintly sarcastic. GeoffYour mate — banter, self-deprecation, somehow useful.

Getting it working

The Coach runs on Groq, a free AI service, and needs a key of your own. It takes two minutes:

  1. Go to console.groq.com/keys, make a free account and create a key. It starts with gsk_.
  2. In BetaLog, open Settings (the cog), tick AI Coach, paste the key into the field, tap Test to check it, then Save.

The key stays on the device you typed it on; it is never synced, so enter it on each device you want the Coach on. Once it is in, the Coach tip widget on the Dashboard also starts working: one sentence a day in your coach's voice.

What it sees

When you tap Analyse, BetaLog sends Groq your name, height and weight if you have entered them, your one-line goal, your active goals with your base grade, and your last 30 days of sessions — the last 14 in detail, including grades, outcomes, exercises, grips, distances and the first 200 characters of each note. The daily tip sends only totals and your three most recent sessions, without notes. Nothing is sent at any other time.

The Coach is told to use the app's own words — base, best — to say no base yet rather than guess, and to give no generic advice. It describes your log; it does not diagnose you. The free tier allows about three analyses a minute, and the app says so if you hit it.

Friends ↑ top

The people icon in the top bar. Friends see each other's climbing, ranked on a board.

Adding a friend

Tap the manage icon on the Friends screen and Generate New Code. You get a code like BL-K7M2X-240926, good for 24 hours; copy it and send it to your friend. They type it into Add a Friend and tap Add. That is it — you are friends both ways at once, with no request to accept. Removing works the same way from a friend's page, with a second tap to confirm, and removes you from their list too.

The board

You and your friends together, your row marked YOU, with a Boulder / Rope switch and two boards:

LevelRanked on the grade owned — Base — then Best. Lower than most people's idea of their grade, and true. Last 30 daysRanked on the hardest send in the last 30 days, then how many. Plain counts, no base needed.

Each row also shows a weekly streak and when they last climbed. Tap a friend for their page: their Base, Best and Flash for each discipline, their pyramid, their sends by grade, their last 30 days, and their five most recent sessions.

Exactly what a friend can see

Only people on your friends list can read this, and it is all they can read:

  • Your name as entered in Settings.
  • For bouldering and for ropes: your Base, Best and Flash over 180 days, your level, your pyramid for the next grade, your attempts, sends and flashes per grade over 180 days, your hardest send and counts over the last 30 days, the date you last climbed, and your all-time hardest send.
  • Your weekly streak.
  • Your five most recent sessions of any kind, as a date, a type and a headline such as 6 climbs, 4 sent or 5 exercises.

Not shared: your notes, where you climbed, your exercise names, your weight, your drinks, your goals, your schedule, your email.

Your data ↑ top

Where it lives. Your log is stored on each device you use, and in your account in the cloud (Google's Firebase, hosted in London). Every change you make is sent to the cloud a moment later, and when you sign in on a device the cloud copy is loaded onto it. The public part your friends see is written at the same time, and only they can read it.

What stays on one device. Your Groq key, your beep timing, your chosen coach and your notification setting are never synced.

Who else sees it.

  • Groq, only when you tap Analyse on the Coach or the daily tip refreshes, and only what Coach describes. Nothing if you have no key.
  • Reminders: if you turn on notifications or the calendar feed, your routine names, days and times go to a small BetaLog service so it can send them. Nothing else about your log does.
  • Feedback goes to a separate shared service, with whatever you type in the form.
  • The site counts visits with Cloudflare's cookieless analytics. No advertising, no tracking across sites.

Taking it with you. Settings → Export JSON gives you the whole log as one file at any time.

Deleting your account. There is no delete button yet. If you want your account and everything in it removed, ask through Send feedback from the account in question and it will be deleted by hand. This page will change when the button exists.

Questions people ask ↑ top

Is it free?Yes. The Coach needs a Groq key, and Groq's free tier is enough. Does it work offline?Logging does, once installed and signed in. Sync, sign-in, the Coach and Friends need a connection. Any gym?Yes. Log what the wall says. BetaLog only compares your climbs with your own, so a soft gym and a hard gym both stay honest. My grade says 6a but I have sent 6bYour Base is the grade you have sent eight times in 180 days; 6b is your Best. Both are on the card. See chapter 5. Why did my goal complete itself?A send counts whenever it happened, so a goal at a grade you had already sent in the window is done the moment it is set — and the app now stops you setting one. My friend says they climb 7aThe board shows Base, the grade owned, not the hardest thing sent once. Switch to Last 30 days for hardest sends. My session movedSessions sit under the date they were given, so editing the date moves the session to that day in History. No calories on my runThe estimate needs a weigh-in on the Health tab dated on or before the run. Where is the delete button?Sessions: History → tap → Delete. Accounts: chapter 12.
