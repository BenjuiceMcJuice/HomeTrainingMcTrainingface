# 2026-09-11

## Grade goals — measured over 90 days, and rated the way weight goals are

Two questions from Ben, looking at Plan › Goals on his phone:

> Can the goal be against the last 90 days not all time? Also any way to build in
> the achieve ability as with the weight?

Both yes. They turned out to be the same piece of work — the achievability rating
needs a *current* grade to measure from, and "current" meaning "best ever" would
have made every rating wrong in the same direction.

---

### 1. "Currently V4" now means the last 90 days

`getCurrentValue('boulder_grade', …)` read the consistent grade over the **whole
log**. That is a career high, not a starting point. It meant:

- the progress bar measured from somewhere the athlete might not have been for two
  years, so distance-to-go read flatteringly short;
- the Goals tab and the Dashboard's own level cards disagreed about what grade Ben
  climbs, because `LevelCard.jsx` had already settled on 90 days with an all-time
  fallback;
- a goal could tick itself off against a season that ended long ago.

Goals now read the same window the Dashboard does. **The fallback matters as much
as the window**: someone three months off the wall has no 90-day consistent grade,
and reporting "No data yet" on their goal would be worse than a stale number. So
`getCurrentValueDetail` returns `{ value, basis }` — `'window'` for a 90-day
reading, `'all'` when it had to fall back — and every surface shows the difference
rather than hiding it:

| Surface | When it falls back |
|---|---|
| Goal card | `Currently V6 (all time)` |
| Coach context | `currently V6 (all time — nothing consistent in the last 90 days)` |
| Auto-achieve | **refuses to fire at all** |

`getCurrentValue` still exists and still returns the bare value, so nothing that
imported it had to change.

#### The bug the fallback surfaced

Seeding a log where every boulder session sat outside the window, the V5 goal
immediately moved itself into **Achieved** — off a V6 season from 15 months ago.
It would have done that before this change too (all-time was the only source), so
it is not a regression, but it is plainly wrong: **achieving a goal is a claim
about now.** `useGoals` now skips the auto-achieve check entirely when a grade
goal's figure came from the fallback. The card still shows full progress and
"At target!"; it just will not tick itself off on stale evidence.

### 2. The achievability rating, for grades

Phase C of the goals spec, built to the same shape as the weight half. Four
signals, deducting from 5:

| Signal | Source | Worst penalty |
|---|---|---|
| **Pace** | grades to go ÷ days left, against how long a grade has actually taken *this* athlete | 2 |
| **Volume** | sessions in that discipline per week over 90 days | 1.25 |
| **Reach** | is anything harder than the current grade being attempted at all | 1 |
| **Schedule debt** | grades gained vs time elapsed since `createdAt` | 1 |

**The pace reference is the interesting part.** `gradeTimeline` replays the
consistent grade — the same ≥3 attempts / ≥40% send rate rule the goal will be
judged by — over a rolling 90-day window at fortnightly steps, collapses the
samples into runs, and reads the days between establishing one grade and
establishing the next. That is this athlete's own rate, measured the same way the
target is, and it beats any table.

When the log holds no grade change there is nothing to measure, and **there is no
published figure for how long a climbing grade takes** — it depends on age,
background, frequency, body, injury and luck, and no study tracks it. So the
fallback is a stated convention, not a finding: 120 days a V-grade, 60 a French
rung (half a V-grade), scaled by `stats.js`'s own level tiers because progress
slowing as grades get harder is the one uncontroversial thing about it. It is
reported through `referenceSource` and the UI says which it is using — "Your own
log: a grade every 140 days across 1 grade change" against "No grade change in
your log yet — compared against a typical 120 days a grade". A default must never
be shown as though it were measured.

**Nothing here ever blocks a goal.** A weight goal is refused above the healthy
ceiling because that is a health risk that is the same for everyone; climbing a
grade faster than usual is not. This is a forecast, not a limit — goals spec,
decision 1.

#### Two rules in `gradeTimeline` that came from real logs, not from theory

- **A regained grade counts.** The first version tracked the running maximum, and
  on the seeded log it reported "no grade change" for an athlete who had clearly
  climbed V3 → V4 — because a V6 season two years earlier had set the high-water
  mark and nothing since could beat it. Caught on the first screenshot, not in
  review. Regaining a grade is the same motion as gaining one for pacing, and for
  a climber coming back it is the only evidence there is.
- **A rise has to stick.** One fortnight above the previous level is a good
  session, not a grade. A rise counts only once the higher grade holds for a
  second sample, or when it is where the log ends. The reference is a median for
  the same reason — a log that yo-yos across a boundary should not flatter itself
  with its shortest crossings.

## Hotfix — climbing less scored better than climbing

Released, same day. Ben, looking at the live Boulder card reading **"Unlikely as
set"**:

> Bouldering unlikely as I've done no bouldering right??

Right — and checking the arithmetic turned up something worse than he was asking
about. Scoring the same goal against an empty log and against a log with one
session in it:

| Log | Score |
|---|---|
| No bouldering at all in 90 days | **3 — "A stretch"** |
| One bouldering session | **2 — "Unlikely as set"** |

**Going bouldering made the goal less achievable.** A monotonicity failure in a
score whose whole job is to reflect effort, shipped that morning.

### The cause

Volume and reach were both charging for the same fact. I had seen the
double-count coming for the *zero*-session case and guarded it —

```js
if (vol.sessions === 0) {
  // Already charged for by volume; saying it twice would swamp the score.
  R.add('reach', 'warn', 'Nothing logged to judge what you are trying', 0)
}
```

— and then failed to apply the same reasoning one band up. At one session in
ninety days, "nothing harder than V4 attempted" is not independent evidence of
timidity; it is "you have not been bouldering", restated. So the empty log paid
volume's 1.25 alone and the one-session log paid 1.25 + 1.

### The fix, and why it is a constant and not another `if`

Suppressing reach whenever volume is bad would have moved the cliff rather than
removed it: volume's *next* band up charges 0.75, and reach can add 1 on top, so
0.6 sessions/week would have totalled 1.75 against the bottom band's 1.25 — the
same bug, ten sessions to the right.

So the bottom band charges `IDLE_PENALTY = 2.25`, which is **defined by the
constraint it has to satisfy**: it must exceed the worst any busier band can
total (0.75 + 1 = 1.75), or the score rewards climbing less. The comment on the
constant says exactly that, because it is the only reason the number is 2.25
rather than a round 2.

Barely climbing is now one fact, charged once, at full weight — and the maximum
penalty falls as volume rises (2.25 → 1.75 → 1.25 → 1), which is monotonicity by
construction rather than by inspection.

### The test that matters

Not the two data points from the bug report — those would pass again the moment
someone adds a fifth factor. The guard is the **property**:

```js
it('never lowers the score as sessions are added, all else equal', () => {
  const scores = [0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 20, 30].map(n => …)
  scores.forEach((s, i) => { if (i) expect(scores[i]).toBeGreaterThanOrEqual(scores[i - 1]) })
})
```

plus one asserting `IDLE_PENALTY > 1.75` directly, so the constraint that sets
the number fails loudly if anyone retunes a band without re-deriving it. The
resulting curve, same goal throughout:

```
 0 sessions | 0   /wk | 2 Unlikely as set
 1 session  | 0.1 /wk | 2 Unlikely as set
 5 sessions | 0.4 /wk | 2 Unlikely as set
 8 sessions | 0.6 /wk | 2 Unlikely as set
13 sessions | 1   /wk | 3 A stretch
30 sessions | 2.3 /wk | 3 A stretch
```

Thirty sessions still caps at 3 because every one of them was at V4 — plenty of
mileage, no reaching. That is the reach factor doing its actual job, now that it
only speaks when there is something to speak about.

### Still open — the label, not the score

"Unlikely as set" is the right *mark* and the wrong *words* for this case. "As
set" points at the target and the date, when the pace is fine (142 days for one
grade against a typical 120) and the real message is "you have not been
bouldering". Nothing needs changing about the goal. Raised with Ben rather than
shipped: it is copy on a screen that works, not a defect.

`npm test` 486 passing, lint 0 errors, build clean, checked running against a
log seeded to match the live one.

## Hotfix 2 — one evening of warm-ups must not redefine your grade

Ben, following up:

> I did one V1 Wednesday.

Which is exactly what the card was reading, and it read it correctly: one session
is below the bar for a 90-day figure, so it fell back to `Currently V4 (all
time)`. But checking *why* it was correct turned up the case one climb away from
it, and that one was not.

### Three V1s would have made him a V1 climber

`calcConsistentGrade` asks for **≥3 attempts at a grade** with a ≥40% send rate.
That is a rule about a grade, not about a window — and one evening of warm-ups
satisfies it on its own. Same log, same day, one extra problem:

| Wednesday | Card reads |
|---|---|
| One V1 | `Currently V4 (all time)` · score 2 |
| **Three V1 warm-ups** | **`Currently V1` · 4 grades to go · score 1** |

And `basis: 'window'`, so it would have been presented as a live measurement with
no caveat. Before this morning's release the all-time figure held V4 — stale, but
never this wrong. **The 90-day change traded a stale reading for a confident
false one, and only the stale half got a label.**

### The same hole fabricated a grade jump

Worse, because it feeds the pace reference. A warm-up-only evening in the middle
of a long V4 run reads as "consistent at V1", which puts a **drop** in the
timeline — and the return to normal then reads as a three-rung **rise**:

```
runs:  V4×19 -> V1×2 -> V4×6
jumps: [{ from: 'V1', to: 'V4', days: 28, daysPerStep: 9 }]
reference median days per grade: 9
```

Nine days a grade. The card would have said *"Your own log: a grade every 9 days
across 1 grade change"* and then rated almost any goal as comfortably paced — the
pace factor silently disabled by a warm-up.

### The fix

One rule, applied in both places that read a window:

```js
/** Sessions needed in a window before a consistent grade read from it means anything. */
var MIN_WINDOW_SESSIONS = 3
```

Consistency is about **repeating a grade across sessions**, so a window holding
one or two of them has not shown anything to be consistent, whatever the
per-grade attempt count says. Counted as *sessions that touched the discipline*,
not climbs — ten problems in one evening is one data point about how you climb,
not ten. Three matches the number the attempt rule already uses.

- `goals.js` — below the threshold the window is skipped entirely and the
  all-time figure is used and **labelled**. Stale and honest beats fresh and
  wrong.
- `gradeGoalScore.js` — `consistentGradeAt` returns null, so a thin window
  produces **no point at all** rather than a confident wrong one. The warm-up day
  now leaves `runs: V4×23`, zero fake jumps, and the reference falls back to the
  stated default.

`MIN_WINDOW_SESSIONS` and `countDisciplineSessions` live in `stats.js` beside
`calcConsistentGrade`, because sample adequacy is a property of that rule rather
than of either caller.

### Most of the work was the fixtures

Seven tests failed on the new rule, and every one of them deserved to: they built
grade eras out of *single sessions* (`consistentSession(300, 'V3')`), which is a
log nobody has. They passed before because the code was as credulous as they
were. Replaced with an `era(startDaysAgo, weeks, grade)` helper that emits weekly
sessions, plus new tests for the trap itself — one evening of warm-ups, three
sessions, and the two-session boundary.

`npm test` 490 passing, lint 0 errors, build clean, checked running against a log
seeded with the three-warm-up case.

## Hotfix 3 — a grade you have already sent is not "a stretch"

Ben, on the live rope card:

> How is 6b+ a stretch when I flashed one today???

He was right, and the object behind the card had the answer sitting in it:

```
volume: { sessionsPerWeek: 0.3, atTarget: 1, sentAtTarget: 1 }
reach:  0  warn  | "Too little logged to judge what you are trying"
```

**`sentAtTarget: 1` and "too little logged to judge what you are trying", in the
same breath.** The app knew he had flashed the target grade that morning and said
it could not tell what he was attempting.

### Cause: this morning's own fix, written one branch too wide

Hotfix 1 suppressed the reach factor in the bottom volume band, to stop the same
*absence* being charged twice. Correct. But it was written as a leading
`if (idle)`, which sits in front of every other branch — including the two
positive ones. So the gag meant for "you have not been climbing" also silenced
"you sent the target".

The reasoning behind the suppression only ever applied to the negative case.
"Nothing harder attempted" really is just "you are not climbing" restated at 0.3
sessions a week. **A send is not.** It is independent evidence at any volume —
the one thing thin mileage cannot argue with.

### Three parts, because fixing the message alone fixes nothing

1. **Reach speaks first.** The positive branches now run ahead of the idle check,
   so a send is never gagged. The gag keeps its original job underneath them —
   there is a test asserting it still fires for the no-send thin-log case.
2. **A send floors the mark.** Reordering alone left the score at 2, because reach
   scores 0 either way and volume's 2.25 still dominated. Everything the scorer
   does above that line is *prediction*; a send at the target settles the question
   the prediction was about. So `SENT_AT_TARGET_FLOOR = 4` — no combination of
   thin mileage or burnt calendar may read below it. It floors rather than fixes
   at 5: sending once on 0.3 sessions a week is a real result and still not the
   same as owning the grade, so the other factors keep their say between 4 and 5.
3. **The card says what changed.** `topReasons` surfaces penalties only, which is
   right while the news is bad and wrong at the moment it turns good — the card
   would have gone from "Unlikely as set" to "Achievable" with nothing on screen
   explaining it. `describeGradeEvidence` returns the send line, rendered in green
   as the one piece of good news on the card.

Before and after, same log, same day:

```
WITHOUT the flash   score 2  Unlikely as set
WITH the flash      score 4  Achievable
                    green:  1 send at 6b+ or harder already — it is about repeatability now
```

### The pattern, three for three

Every defect in this feature today has been the same shape: **a rule that was
right about the case I had in mind and wrong about the case next to it.**
Running-max was right about career highs and wrong about regaining a grade. The
per-grade attempt count was right about a grade and wrong about a window. The
idle gag was right about absence and wrong about evidence. In each case the code
was confident where it should have asked what else the branch could catch.

`npm test` 495 passing, lint 0 errors, build clean, checked running against a log
seeded to match the live card.
