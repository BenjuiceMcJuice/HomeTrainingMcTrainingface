# 2026-09-11

## Grade goals — measured over 90 days, and rated the way weight goals are

Two questions from Ben, looking at Plan › Goals on his phone:

> Can the goal be against the last 90 days not all time? Also any way to build in
> the achieve ability as with the weight?

Both yes. They turned out to be the same piece of work — the achievability rating
needs a *current* grade to measure from, and "current" meaning "best ever" would
have made every rating wrong in the same direction.

---

### 1. "Currently V4" now means the last 90 days

`getCurrentValue('boulder_grade', …)` read the consistent grade over the **whole
log**. That is a career high, not a starting point. It meant:

- the progress bar measured from somewhere the athlete might not have been for two
  years, so distance-to-go read flatteringly short;
- the Goals tab and the Dashboard's own level cards disagreed about what grade Ben
  climbs, because `LevelCard.jsx` had already settled on 90 days with an all-time
  fallback;
- a goal could tick itself off against a season that ended long ago.

Goals now read the same window the Dashboard does. **The fallback matters as much
as the window**: someone three months off the wall has no 90-day consistent grade,
and reporting "No data yet" on their goal would be worse than a stale number. So
`getCurrentValueDetail` returns `{ value, basis }` — `'window'` for a 90-day
reading, `'all'` when it had to fall back — and every surface shows the difference
rather than hiding it:

| Surface | When it falls back |
|---|---|
| Goal card | `Currently V6 (all time)` |
| Coach context | `currently V6 (all time — nothing consistent in the last 90 days)` |
| Auto-achieve | **refuses to fire at all** |

`getCurrentValue` still exists and still returns the bare value, so nothing that
imported it had to change.

#### The bug the fallback surfaced

Seeding a log where every boulder session sat outside the window, the V5 goal
immediately moved itself into **Achieved** — off a V6 season from 15 months ago.
It would have done that before this change too (all-time was the only source), so
it is not a regression, but it is plainly wrong: **achieving a goal is a claim
about now.** `useGoals` now skips the auto-achieve check entirely when a grade
goal's figure came from the fallback. The card still shows full progress and
"At target!"; it just will not tick itself off on stale evidence.

### 2. The achievability rating, for grades

Phase C of the goals spec, built to the same shape as the weight half. Four
signals, deducting from 5:

| Signal | Source | Worst penalty |
|---|---|---|
| **Pace** | grades to go ÷ days left, against how long a grade has actually taken *this* athlete | 2 |
| **Volume** | sessions in that discipline per week over 90 days | 1.25 |
| **Reach** | is anything harder than the current grade being attempted at all | 1 |
| **Schedule debt** | grades gained vs time elapsed since `createdAt` | 1 |

**The pace reference is the interesting part.** `gradeTimeline` replays the
consistent grade — the same ≥3 attempts / ≥40% send rate rule the goal will be
judged by — over a rolling 90-day window at fortnightly steps, collapses the
samples into runs, and reads the days between establishing one grade and
establishing the next. That is this athlete's own rate, measured the same way the
target is, and it beats any table.

When the log holds no grade change there is nothing to measure, and **there is no
published figure for how long a climbing grade takes** — it depends on age,
background, frequency, body, injury and luck, and no study tracks it. So the
fallback is a stated convention, not a finding: 120 days a V-grade, 60 a French
rung (half a V-grade), scaled by `stats.js`'s own level tiers because progress
slowing as grades get harder is the one uncontroversial thing about it. It is
reported through `referenceSource` and the UI says which it is using — "Your own
log: a grade every 140 days across 1 grade change" against "No grade change in
your log yet — compared against a typical 120 days a grade". A default must never
be shown as though it were measured.

**Nothing here ever blocks a goal.** A weight goal is refused above the healthy
ceiling because that is a health risk that is the same for everyone; climbing a
grade faster than usual is not. This is a forecast, not a limit — goals spec,
decision 1.

#### Two rules in `gradeTimeline` that came from real logs, not from theory

- **A regained grade counts.** The first version tracked the running maximum, and
  on the seeded log it reported "no grade change" for an athlete who had clearly
  climbed V3 → V4 — because a V6 season two years earlier had set the high-water
  mark and nothing since could beat it. Caught on the first screenshot, not in
  review. Regaining a grade is the same motion as gaining one for pacing, and for
  a climber coming back it is the only evidence there is.
- **A rise has to stick.** One fortnight above the previous level is a good
  session, not a grade. A rise counts only once the higher grade holds for a
  second sample, or when it is where the log ends. The reference is a median for
  the same reason — a log that yo-yos across a boundary should not flatter itself
  with its shortest crossings.
