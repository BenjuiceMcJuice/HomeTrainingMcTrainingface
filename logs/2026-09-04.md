## The CORS allowlist never covered preview deploys

Found while writing the deploy runbook, not by anything failing — which is the only reason it was
found before Ben hit it.

`ALLOWED_ORIGINS` in both Workers listed `https://betalog.pages.dev` but **not** the per-branch
preview deploys Cloudflare actually builds, which come out as `<branch-slug>.betalog.pages.dev`. The
subscribe call is a `PUT` with a JSON content type, so it is a non-simple request: the browser sends
a preflight `OPTIONS` first, the Worker answers without an `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` header
because the origin is not in the list, and the request never leaves. Turning notifications on from a
branch preview would have failed with nothing useful on screen.

This was inherited, not new — I copied the list from `betalog-calendar` when writing `betalog-push`,
so **both** Workers had the same gap. Route A has presumably only ever been enabled from production
or localhost, which is why it never showed up. Worth stating plainly: the whole point of a preview
deploy is checking a change on a real URL before releasing it, and for anything touching a Worker
that has not actually been possible.

### The fix

`workers/shared/cors.js` — one module, imported by both Workers, holding the exact origins, the
preview pattern and the `corsHeaders` builder. Both Workers now call it; neither keeps its own copy.
The duplication is what let them drift into being wrong the same way, so removing the second copy
matters as much as widening the first.

The preview pattern is anchored at both ends and its subdomain label cannot contain a dot:

    /^https:\/\/[a-z0-9][a-z0-9-]*\.betalog\.pages\.dev$/

That was chosen against the two shapes that would matter if it were sloppy — a lookalike someone
else could register (`evil-betalog.pages.dev`, no dot before `betalog`) and a suffix attack
(`betalog.pages.dev.evil.com`). Both are now covered by tests rather than by my reading of the
regex.

**Verified:** 12 new origin checks in `workers/betalog-push/scripts/smoke-test.mjs`, 30 checks in
that file in total, all passing. Both Workers bundle — `betalog-push` 19.00 KiB, `betalog-calendar`
3.10 KiB. App untouched: 246 tests, build green, lint 0 errors.

**Not merged.** Same reason as yesterday — and note the Worker change would not ship on a merge
anyway. Workers deploy only via `wrangler deploy`, never from a Cloudflare Pages build.

## The weight widget gets a chart, chips and its goal on the axis

**Why it had no dropdown:** it was left behind. The widget system spec's own timeframe table
(`docs/specs/betalog_widget_system_spec.md`) gives Weight a `30d / 90d` window, but phases B and D
only ever wired cardio, gym, alcohol and the two level cards. Weight kept its hardcoded 30-day
average and stayed the one widget on the Dashboard with no body at all — no `WidgetShell`, no chips,
no chart — which is exactly the collapse exception finding 5 was supposed to remove.

### Bodyweight cannot use the bar chart

`BarTimeline` measures every bar from zero. That is right for minutes trained and units drunk, and
useless for weight: at 78 kg, a year of weigh-ins is a row of identical bars. So there are now two
chart shapes, and the split is by what the number *is*:

| | totals | levels |
|---|---|---|
| geometry | `lib/barChart.js` | `lib/trendChart.js` |
| renderer | `BarTimeline` | `TrendTimeline` |
| bucketing | `buildValueTimeline` (sums) | `buildAverageTimeline` (averages) |
| baseline | zero, always | zoomed onto the data |

`buildAverageTimeline` is the summing timeline with the aggregation swapped, sharing
`buildBucketScaffold`, so the weight chart covers the same span and labels its axis the same way as
every other card. Two deliberate differences from summing: an empty bucket keeps `avg: null` rather
than 0 — "did not weigh in" is not "weighed nothing" — and a record with no reading on it is skipped
rather than counted, which is the opposite of the summing timeline, where an empty session still
counts as a session.

### Three rules keep the zoomed axis honest

A zoomed axis lies easily, so `buildTrendGeometry` is constrained:

1. **A floor of 2 kg on the span.** 300 g of daily noise on a full-height chart reads as a crisis.
   Under the floor the range opens around the midpoint and the line goes flat — which is what
   actually happened.
2. **Bounds snap outward to 0.5 kg**, so the three axis ticks are numbers you can read a value off,
   not the exact min and max of the data.
3. **The target only joins the scale when it can share it** — at most 3× the data's own span. A goal
   20 kg away would squash a month of weigh-ins into one band; past that the caller is told
   (`targetShown: false`) and the card says "Goal 62 kg is off this scale" instead of drawing a line
   nobody can use. The card asks the same geometry rather than re-deriving the rule, so the caption
   cannot disagree with the chart.

Gaps are bridged rather than broken. A fortnight without a weigh-in is a gap in the record, not a
change in bodyweight, and at 30 daily buckets a line that stops dead at every missed day is
unreadable. The dots say where the readings actually are.

### What the card does now

- **Chips `30d / 90d / 12m`**, persisted per card like every other widget. The spec said `30d / 90d`,
  from when 30d was just the window one average was taken over; with a chart on it, 12m is the window
  that answers "where is this going", and offering the same three as the other charts is the point of
  rule 2. Default stays 30d.
- **Header unchanged** — weight, BMI pill, diff vs the window average, goal progress. So collapsing
  the card leaves exactly what production shows today, and the chart is the only thing the fold hides.
- **`weight` is deliberately not in `COLLAPSE_DEFAULTS`.** The other chart widgets fold by default;
  this one is a 56px line under a header that already fills the card, and folding it by default would
  ship a chart nobody would see. `widgetCollapse.test.js` already asserted weight is glanceable — the
  test was right.
- Tap a point for that bucket's readout ("week of 20/7 · 77.4 kg avg of 2 weigh-ins"), and a line
  under the chart for the window: count, range, and the change first-to-last.

## Weight goals also say what percentage of you is left

Follow-up to the widget above, and display-only by decision: goals are still entered in kg, and the
data model is untouched. What changed is that "1.4 kg to lose" now reads "1.4 kg (1.7%) to lose".

The reason to bother: the same kg is a different ask at different body sizes. Side by side in the
harness, 1.4 kg is **1.7%** at 80.4 kg and **2.3%** at 59.6 kg — the kg figure alone never says which
one you are, and the percentage is the half of the pair that stays comparable across a long cut or
between two people.

`pctOfBodyweight(deltaKg, bodyKg)` in `stats.js` is the whole of it — pure, one decimal, sign dropped
because the label around it already says lose or gain. It measures against the **current** weight,
not the goal's `startValue`: the question is how much of you is left to lose, not what share of a
weight you may be nowhere near any more. It returns null rather than a nonsense percentage, and tests
for null/undefined/'' *before* converting, since `Number(null)` is 0 and a missing difference is not
a difference of nothing.

Two call sites, deliberately worded the same: the weight card's goal line
(`Goal: 79 kg · 1.4 kg (1.7%) to lose`) and `toGoLabel` in Plan › Goals
(`Currently 80.4 kg · 1.4 kg (1.7%) to go`). Only the `weight` goal type takes the suffix — a grade
or a distance has no body to be a percentage of.

### Then both numbers, not one

Asked for the size of the goal as well as what is left, so the goal line carries two percentages that
answer different questions:

    Goal: 79 kg (4.8% cut) · 1.4 kg (1.7%) to lose

The **cut** is start → target as a share of the starting weight. It is fixed for the life of the goal
— it describes the ask. The **remaining** percentage moves as you do. Same pair in Plan › Goals,
except the cut rides the title row (`Bodyweight  4.8% cut`) rather than the status row, which already
carries the current weight, the distance left and the days, and would have wrapped on a narrow phone.

Wording follows the direction: `cut` when the target is below the start, `gain` when above.

**Verified:** 286 tests pass (5 new), build and lint clean, and both screens rendered in a browser
across four cases — losing, the same kg on a smaller frame, a gaining goal, and a 320px-wide phone to
check the longer line. The card was also quoting "6.0 kg" where Goals said "6 kg"; it now uses the
card's own `kg()` trim, so the two screens agree.

## Weight goals now show the pace they ask for — and refuse an unhealthy one

A weight goal is a distance and a date, which together are a rate nobody was being shown. The card
now says `Lose 0.7 kg/wk · 3 kg/month · 14d left`, and the goal sheet will not save a loss that is
too fast.

### What counts as too fast, and why

`lib/weightRate.js` holds the rule. Two ceilings, the tighter one wins:

- **1 kg/week absolute** — NHS guidance is 0.5–1 kg a week off roughly a 600 kcal deficit, and is
  explicit that heavier restriction without supervision risks missing nutrients.
- **1% of bodyweight per week** — because a flat kg figure is the wrong shape for a climbing app:
  1 kg/week off 55 kg is nearly twice the ask it is off 100 kg. Garthe et al. (2011) ran elite
  athletes at 0.7%/week against 1.4%/week for the same total loss; the slow group gained lean mass
  and improved countermovement jump and bench press, the fast group did not.

Bands: **steady** ≤0.7%, **brisk** 0.7–1%, **too fast** above that or over 1 kg/wk — blocked.

**0.7% is the top of steady, not a warning line.** The first cut had steady at 0.5%, and the screenshot
made the mistake obvious: at 0.58%/week the card said "sustainable, but ~0.7% is where strength holds
up best", which reads as *go faster*. It is the last rate with nothing to warn about, not the first
with something — and it is a ceiling rather than a target: Garthe compared 0.7 against 1.4 and nothing
slower, so it shows what 1.4%/week costs, not that 0.7% beats 0.5%. Slower is fine, and generally at
least as good for body composition.

**Gains are flagged, never blocked.** Lean gain past ~0.5%/week is unrealistic, not unsafe, and
refusing to let someone set it would be the app overreaching. The hard stop is for the health risk
that comes with rapid loss.

### Two bugs the tests caught before the screen did

**`Number(null)` is 0 again.** A blank target parsed as "0 kg", so an empty goal sheet computed a
catastrophic loss and would have opened on a red block before anything was typed. Same trap as
`pctOfBodyweight` this morning; the fix is the same, and both now test for null *before* converting.

**The band was decided on more precision than the screen shows.** Exactly 0.7%/week came out as
0.7000000000000001 and got flagged for exceeding 0.7, while the card displayed "0.7%". The verdict is
now taken on the rounded figure, so what is judged is what is read.

### Scope

Rate only. Nothing here judges whether the *target weight* is sensible for the person — a BMI floor on
the target is a separate question (and a real one in climbing, given RED-S). Existing goals are not
migrated: one saved before the rule, or overtaken by its date, still shows, in red, with what it is
asking for.

**Verified:** 304 tests pass (18 new for `weightRate`), build and lint clean. Both screens driven in a
browser — steady, brisk, too-fast and past-date cards, and the goal sheet checked to confirm Save is
enabled at steady and brisk, disabled when too fast, and that clicking it then does nothing.

## Backlogged: the coach has the goals and is never asked about them

Checked before writing it up, because the goals spec claims goals are "injected into the AI Coach
context so Groq can give targeted advice" and that reads as done. Half of it is: `buildContext` sends

Written into the goals spec as a backlog item with three parts: a per-goal verdict in the JSON; the

## Weight goal achievability, worked out on a real goal

`lib/weightGoalScore.js` — pure, 24 tests, **not wired to any screen**. Scores a weight goal 1–5 and
returns the reasons, alongside the calorie picture. Built against the goal on the phone (95.8 kg,
186 cm, 85 kg by 31 Oct) rather than an invented example, which is what turned up the flaw below.

**Why a second module rather than more of `weightRate.js`:** they answer different questions.
`weightRate` asks whether a rate is *healthy* — physiology, the same for everyone at a given
bodyweight, and it owns blocking. This asks whether *this athlete on this evidence* will do it, and
the answer lives in their log. Two goals can ask for the same safe 0.6 kg/week and be worlds apart.

**Four signals, scored by deduction from 5.** Deducting rather than adding keeps 5 meaning "nothing
here argues against it": track record (best sustained 4-week loss in the log — the signal nothing else
in the app has), trend (last 30 days), headroom (required rate vs the healthy ceiling), schedule debt
(progress vs time elapsed).

**The flaw the real numbers exposed.** First run charged 1.0 for "trend going up" *and* 1.0 for
"further from the target than when set" — one fact, paid for twice, swamping everything else. Schedule
debt now takes half penalty when progress is negative, because the trend factor already owns
direction. An invented test case would not have caught it; both factors only collide when someone has
actually gained since setting a goal.

**Calories.** The deficit itself needs no assumptions (kg/wk × 7700 ÷ 7). What it *means* needs
maintenance, and the profile has no age or sex — so `estimateMaintenance` takes them optionally,
defaults to age 35 and the midpoint of the two Mifflin-St Jeor constants, derives activity from logged
sessions per week, and returns its `assumptions` array so a screen can show them rather than pretend.
Two flags survive the ±15%: deficit as a share of maintenance, and intake below estimated RMR.

**`counterOffer` compounds.** 0.7% of a falling bodyweight over 8 weeks is 90.5 kg, not the 91.0 a flat
0.67 kg/week subtraction gives. The naive version overstates by more the longer the cut.

### The result on the real goal

    score 1 — Not achievable as set
    1.33 kg/wk = 1.39% bw/wk · 1,463 kcal/day deficit · 52% of estimated maintenance
    implied intake ~1,338 kcal/day, under an estimated RMR of ~1,868
    best month in the log: 0.35 kg/wk · 30d trend: +0.61 kg/wk · 34% of the window gone, none of the weight

Set fresh today, both counter-offers score **3**: 90.5 kg by 31 Oct (0.65 kg/wk, ~715 kcal/day) or
85 kg by 1 Feb (0.5 kg/wk, ~550 kcal/day). Neither reaches 4 while the trend is upward and the log
holds no month faster than 0.35 kg/wk — which is the model working, not a bug: the honest answer is
that the first thing to establish is a month of losing anything at all.

**Open before it goes on a screen:** where it shows; that a low score must never block (`weightRate`
owns blocking, on health grounds — this is advice); and that editing a goal keeps the original
`startValue` and `createdAt`, so a re-set goal carries the old baseline and scores worse than the same
goal set fresh.

**Verified:** 328 tests pass (24 new), build and lint clean.

three lines of red prose in a glanceable widget was too much, and that decision stands. So the widget
gets a **mark, not a sentence** — five dots on the existing rate line, filled to the score — and every
word lives on the goal.

Dots rather than "1/5": a bare number next to `1.33 kg/wk · 5.8 kg/month · 57d left` reads as a fifth
quantity. Dots read as a rating and cost no words. No tap target either — `WidgetShell` forbids
interactive elements in a header that is itself the collapse control.

Plan › Goals gets the mark, the label and **at most two reasons, worst first, and only at 3 or below**
— at 4–5 there is nothing to say and a card should not manufacture concern.

The goal sheet is where it earns its place: live as the target and date change, turning the form into
a tuner. Below it, when the score is 2 or less, `counterOffer` as **one-tap buttons** — one holding the
date and moving the target, one holding the target and moving the date. Tapping fills the fields, it
does not save. A refusal without an alternative is just a refusal.

### Two decisions taken in the spec

**The score never blocks.** `weightRate.blocked` remains the only thing that disables Save, on health
grounds. A 1/5 goal inside the healthy rate is fully settable — the app thinks it is a poor bet, and
that is the user's call. This is the reason the two live in separate files.

**Changing the target re-baselines the goal; changing only the date does not.** `updateGoal` keeps
the original `startValue` and `createdAt`, so after an edit the progress bar measures from a weight
the athlete may be nowhere near and the score carries schedule debt earned against a target that no
longer exists. A new target is a new goal. Cost: the original baseline is lost, and there is no goal
history feature to lose it from.

### One thing to fix before it ships, not after

`demonstratedLossRate` is O(n²) over the weigh-in log and would run on every Dashboard render. Three
years of daily weighing is ~1,100 entries and 1.2M pair comparisons per render. Bound the scan to the
last ~400 days or memoise per goal — cheap now, a bug report later.

## Built: the achievability score on screen (phase A)

Three surfaces, one number, words only where there is room for them.

- **Dashboard weight card** — five dots on the end of the rate line, nothing else. `ScoreDots` in
  `components/ui/`, decorative with an `aria-label`; never a control, because `WidgetShell` forbids
  interactive elements in a header row that is itself the collapse button.
- **Plan › Goals** — dots, label, and up to two reasons.
- **Goal sheet** — the same, live as the target and date change. Retyping the target and watching the
  dots fill is the whole point of putting it here.

### Three things the rendered version changed

**Duplicated reasoning.** The goal card printed "Healthy loss tops out around 0.96 kg a week for you"
and then, two lines later, "Needs 1.33 kg/wk, above the 0.96 kg/wk healthy ceiling". `topReasons` now
takes an `exclude` list and both surfaces pass `['headroom']` — which also frees the slot for a reason
that says something new. With it excluded, the real goal reads: *asks for more than twice the best
month logged (0.35 kg/wk) · weight is going up, not down.*

**The score sits above the rate in the sheet**, not below: the verdict should read before the
arithmetic that produced it.

**Training frequency is measured, not assumed.** Both screens count sessions in the last 30 days and
pass `sessionsPerWeek` into the maintenance estimate. An activity dropdown is a question nobody
answers honestly, and the log already knows.

### Decision 2, implemented

Changing a goal's **target** now re-baselines `startValue` to the current value and `createdAt` to
today; changing only the **date** does not. Verified in a browser: editing 85 → 92 kg moved the
baseline from 94.3 to 95.8. Without it the progress bar keeps measuring from a weight the athlete may
be nowhere near, and the score carries schedule debt earned against a target that no longer exists.
Applies to every goal type, guarded on `getCurrentValue` returning something.

### The perf bound, before it shipped rather than after

`demonstratedLossRate` now looks back at most 400 days. Three years of daily weighing is ~1,100
entries and 1.2M pair comparisons *per render* of a Dashboard card; the cap keeps it in the low
thousands, and a good month from four years ago is not evidence about this month anyway. The limit is
a parameter, so the full history is still reachable, and there is a test that a 1,100-entry log
scores in under 120ms.

**Verified:** 334 tests pass (5 new), build and lint clean, all three surfaces rendered at scores 1, 3
and 5, plus the re-baseline check driven in a browser.
