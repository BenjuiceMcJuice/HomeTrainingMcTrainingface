# 2026-09-06

## The corner wedges are out; the type mark is a chip

Shipped the corner wedges to prod yesterday. Ben, on seeing them on his phone: *"I don't like the
triangle things. They look rubbish. How about another option. I want all widgets to look the same in
style but different by type. The smaller icons are good."*

Fair. The wedge solved the width problem — that half was right and is untouched — but as a *mark* it
read as a dog-eared corner rather than something designed. The small inline icons he likes are the
part that stays.

### The rule, and what it changed about the code

"Same in style, different by type" is a constraint on where the decision lives, not just on how it
looks. It was being made nine times, once per card, in nine slightly different bits of JSX. So
`WidgetCorner.jsx` became **`WidgetMark.jsx`**, and every card now hands it an icon and an accent
and gets back a mark. Two exports, because the mark has two halves:

- `WidgetMark` — the icon, inline at the head of the headline row.
- `WidgetEdge` — an optional absolutely-positioned card flourish. `position: relative` on the card
  is all it asks for.

A `VARIANT` constant at the top picks the look, so changing the whole dashboard is one word.

### Four options, rendered rather than described

Built all four for real and screenshotted them on the same cards:

- **A `chip`** — icon in a 22px rounded tile of its own accent at 12%. Uniform geometry, colour and
  glyph by type.
- **B `spine`** — bare icon, 3px accent bar down the card's left edge.
- **C `rule`** — bare icon, 3px accent bar across the card's top edge.
- **D `bare`** — accent-coloured icon, nothing else.

Set to **`chip`** as the recommendation and pushed for Ben to pick from a preview. C is too loud
stacked nine deep — it reads as a striped list. B reads as an alert callout. D is clean but the type
signal is faint. A is the one that looks deliberate at nine-in-a-column.

The other three stay live in the file rather than being deleted: the choice is taste, and taste
changes.
