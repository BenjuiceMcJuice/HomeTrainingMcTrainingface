### The diagnosis

Yesterday's three defects were not three mistakes. They were **one abstraction
failing three times.** `calcConsistentGrade` crushes a whole log into a single
grade, and every fix since has been an attempt to smuggle a discarded piece back
in — four tuning constants in a day, each existing so that one number could not
lie in one particular way.

The log, meanwhile, is already pyramid-shaped: one row per climb with a grade, an
outcome and a date. `GradeChart` even draws it. Nothing reads it.

### The research, and what it will not support

Checked rather than assumed, since the design rests on it.

- **The pyramid is standard and old.** Hörst, *How to Climb 5.12*; now common
  coaching material. Canonical shape **1 · 2 · 4 · 8**, 2:1 per tier, base at a
  grade you send consistently.
- **Progression does slow sharply.** Beginner V0 → V2 in months; V4–V6 roughly
  6–12 months a grade; past V8, years. `LEVEL_FACTOR` points the right way.
- **Not claimed:** the widely-copied "slightly more than a tripling in difficulty
  per V-grade". That is a blog summary, not a measurement, and no arithmetic
  rests on it. The literature justifies a *shape*, not a constant.
- **The caveat:** Power Company's point that lower tiers are about *variety*, not
  count. BetaLog cannot see variety, so every figure counts sends, not distinct
  problems, and says so.

### Two things found in the data model

- **`Climb.attempts` is dead** — typed as a number, hardcoded to `1` at the only
  call site. One tap is one climb; counts come from rows.
- **`Climb.routeId` is always null**, so six sends of the same V4 are
  indistinguishable from six different ones. A pyramid is explicitly about
  distinct climbs. This is the one real gap, and it shaped the design.

### Phase 1 — `lib/pyramid.js`, wired to nothing

Three readings replace one number: **Project** (hardest sent, ≥1), **Working**
(hardest genuinely tried, ≥3 attempts — deliberately the same 3
`calcConsistentGrade` already uses), **Base** (hardest grade whose own pyramid is
complete, defined by reusing `pyramidReadiness` rather than a fresh threshold).

Three rules do the work: **surplus spills downward** (ten 6c+ sends demonstrate
the 6c tier; one 7a send fills only the top tier), **one session cannot fill a
tier** (`MAX_SENDS_PER_SESSION`, the cheapest honest defence against laps given no
route identity), and **no minimum-evidence guard is needed** — a thin log makes
empty tiers, which is the truth.

Readiness is `credited / required`, and `score = 1 + pct × 4`. **The scale is the
completeness, so there is nothing to tune.**

#### The bug phase 1 found in itself

A V2 pyramid has only V1 and V0 beneath it, so the four-tier shape cannot be
built. Unhandled, V2 "completed" on three tiers and `baseGrade` returned **V2 for
a climber working V4** — a lower bar at the bottom of the ladder, making the
reading incomparable. Now `truncated: true`, never `complete`; beginners have no
Base and callers show Working or Project. Found by a failing test whose
expectation was also wrong, which took some care to separate.

#### What it says on a realistic log

```
TARGET 6b+   Pyramid complete   (5/5, 100% built)
TARGET 6c    Base nearly there  (4/5,  87% built)   next up: 6b+ (1 more)
TARGET 7a    No base yet        (1/5,   7% built)   next up: 6b+ (7 more)
```

Three goals, three honest and actionable answers, one model, no special cases.
Against yesterday's "Unlikely as set" plus four constants, this is the argument
for the rewrite.

### The open question phase 1 exists to answer

**Is the four-tier shape too demanding for real logs?** A textbook 2/4/8 at
V4/V3/V2 still fails "own V4" at 14/15, because the shape wants eight sends at V1
and nobody logs warm-ups. Either the shape is right and most people's Base is
genuinely lower than they think — which is what the coaching literature actually
claims — or `PYRAMID_SHAPE` wants softening for logged-data reality. Wants
checking against Ben's real log before phase 2 touches a screen.

## Phase 1, revised — Ben on pyramid depth

> I think you're right that V2 and below probably don't need to factor that much as
> most people can do a V2 first couple of tries and these low level ones won't get
> logged. Also. The harder you climb this is likely to happen for V3s also. Almost
> like the pyramid can only ever be x rows in depth based on what your current
> 'grade' is if that makes sense?

It makes sense, it is right, and it turned out to be two separate findings once the
numbers were run. Phase 1 exists to be wrong in private, and it was — twice.

### 1. Depth capped at three tiers

Measured against a log whose hardest send was **V4**:

| Depth | "ready for V6" | "ready for V7" |
|---|---|---|
| 4 tiers | 80% | **53%** |
| 3 tiers | 33% | **0%** |

53% ready for V7 having never sent a V5 is nonsense. The fourth tier sits in warm-up
territory, so it is *simultaneously* never logged and trivially filled by volume that
means nothing — harsh on honest logs and far too generous on ambitious goals at the
same time. `PYRAMID_MAX_DEPTH = 3`.

**This reverses yesterday's truncation rule.** I had made a short pyramid at the
bottom of the ladder disqualifying, to stop `base` dropping to V2 for a V4 climber.
Under Ben's framing that was treating the symptom: a shallow pyramid at a low grade is
the *correct* shape. `truncated` is still reported, no longer disqualifying.

### 2. Readiness is built from the bottom, stopping at the first gap

Capping the depth exposed the next layer. The same V4 climber still read **57% ready
for V6**, because the V4 row was full while V5 and V6 were empty — total-material
counting let the easy tiers carry an absent top.

A pyramid with a hole in it is not partly built; it is built *up to the hole*. That is
true of real pyramids and it is what makes the figure discriminate:
`pct = solidTiers / depth`, counting consecutive met tiers upward from the base. Total
material survives as `fillPct`, and `topTierMet` / `sentTarget` stay separate so a
send at the target is never hidden.

### 3. `baseGrade` was answering the wrong question

Found while checking the above. It was "the hardest grade whose pyramid is complete" —
but readiness asks *can I get there*, and a single send on a broad base answers yes.
On a well-logged V4/V5 season with one V6 in it, **Base reported V6**.

Ownership is a different question: have you done it repeatedly? Base now reads the
spread — hardest grade with `credited ≥ max(shape)` — and answers **V4** on that log.
Twenty-four V4 sends against four V5s and one V6 is what a coach would say.

### Where it landed

```
SOLID — a V4/V5 season with one V6 in it
  project V6 · working V5 · base V4
  V5: 100%  Pyramid complete    V5 1/1   V4 2/2   V3 4/4
  V6: 100%  Pyramid complete    V6 1/1   V5 2/2   V4 4/4
  V7:  33%  Base thin           V7 0/1*  V6 1/2*  V5 4/4

SPARSE — hardest send V4
  project V4 · working V4 · base V4
  V5:  67%  Base nearly there   V5 0/1*  V4 2/2   V3 4/4
  V6:  33%  Base thin           V6 0/1*  V5 0/2*  V4 4/4
  V7:   0%  No base yet         V7 0/1*  V6 0/2*  V5 0/4*
```

Six answers, one model, no special cases. The sparse climber is told plainly that V5
is nearly there and V7 is not a conversation yet.

### Still open

- Should depth **vary** with the target's height rather than being flat three? Ben's

  than below V3. Flat three is what the evidence supports; a curve would be invented

- Does this describe a **real** log better than the number does? Still the question

## Depth: three, then back to four — and why the first answer was wrong

Ben, on the two open questions:

> re 1- depth, lets go with what internet researched climbing evidence (if thats the
> evidence you were referring to) suggests.

The parenthetical was a fair challenge, and the answer is **no, it was not**. The
evidence for capping depth at three was my own probe, not the literature — and worse,
it does not survive its own follow-up.

The 53%-ready-for-V7 figure that justified the cap was measured **before** readiness
was rebuilt from the bottom. It was the total-material arithmetic doing the
flattering, not the fourth tier. Re-measured under bottom-up counting:

| Depth | SPARSE log (project V4): V6 | V7 |
|---|---|---|
| 3 tiers | 33% | 0% |
| 4 tiers | 50% | **25%**, not 53% |

So the cut was solving a problem that the other fix had already solved. Reverted to
`PYRAMID_MAX_DEPTH = 4`, the canonical 1·2·4·8.

**Ben's point was still right and still load-bearing** — it is what led to finding the
counting bug. But the correct answer to "warm-up grades don't get logged" turns out to
be **surplus spill**, which was already in the model: a climber with 12 sends at V3
covers the V2 tier they never logged, while a climber with no volume has nothing to
spill and stays honest. Two tests now hold both halves.

Sequence worth remembering: a fix was justified by a measurement taken with a broken
instrument, and only re-measuring after repairing the instrument showed the fix was
unnecessary. Cheap to catch here because phase 1 is wired to nothing.

Phases 3 and 4 are specced but not built, in the order Ben put them.

### The part worth arguing about before it gets built

He asked for a **% chance of reaching grade XYZ**. §7 says plainly that this cannot be
an honest probability. `pct` is a *measurement* — how much of the base exists. A chance
of success is a *prediction*, and converting one to the other needs an outcome dataset
(many climbers, known pyramids, known results) that does not exist and cannot be
derived from one person's log. Inventing the mapping would be the same mistake as the
"tripling in difficulty" figure this spec already refuses to build on.

What *is* computable end to end from the log: **shortfall** (have it), **fill rate**
(credited sends per month at the relevant grades), and **conversion time** (how long
this athlete has taken to turn a full base into a send at the next grade — `gradeTimeline`
already measures exactly this). Those give a **projected ready date**, and comparing it
with the deadline gives a margin in weeks.

Recommendation: lead with the date and the margin.

> Ready around **mid-March** at your current rate. Your deadline is **1 December**.

It answers the deadline sensitivity Ben asked for with no extra machinery — the same
pyramid against a one-week and a three-month deadline differs only in the margin — and
the coaching line comes free from `nextUp` ("get 3 more V4s logged"). If a single
figure is still wanted it should be labelled *confidence*, not chance, and documented
as not a probability. Flagged so the decision is explicit rather than accidental.

### A framing rule, from Ben mid-turn

> also my data is not so complete and I'm getting back after recovering so I know I
> could do more I'm limiting myself on purpose (but this may also be a factor but lets
> not overcomplicate for now)

This is more than a caveat — it sets the copy rule for every later phase, so it is §5
of the spec rather than a footnote. **A climber deliberately holding back produces
exactly the same pyramid as one who has hit a ceiling, and the model cannot tell them
apart.** Everything it outputs therefore describes *the log*, never the climber:
"nothing logged at V5 yet", never "you can't do V5".

Not building a deload or return-from-injury mode — it would need input nobody wants to
give, and honest wording covers most of the harm. Noted as deliberately unmodelled.

## The honesty principle, fleshed out for a coach

> so tbh this doesn't have to be perfect as tbh it's mostly me using the app...
> however... this principle could be fleshed out more for a climbing coach.

Two instructions in one sentence: **don't gold-plate the pyramid**, and **do take the
"describes the log, not the climber" rule seriously**, because it stops being a
courtesy the moment a second person reads the data.

New doc: `docs/specs/betalog_data_honesty_spec.md`. No code, no computation, no
settings screen — it is a writing and readout standard.

### Why the coach case is the sharp one

Checked the vision doc rather than inventing a coach model: **coaching profiles** are
already a Partner-tier feature and **coach notes on member sessions (opt-in)** is in
the backlog. So a third party reading an athlete's data is a real planned direction.

The asymmetry is the whole argument. Ben reading his own thin pyramid *already knows*
why it is thin — recovering, holding back, not logging warm-ups — and supplies that
context silently without noticing. A coach has none of it. The output **is** the whole
picture, it **is** acted on, and it **carries authority** with the athlete being shown
it. So the same reading that is harmlessly incomplete for the owner is confidently
wrong about someone who is not in the room.

### The six rules

1. **Absence is not evidence** — "no V5 sends" and "tried V5 nine times, never sent"
   are opposite facts that both read as an empty tier. The model already separates
   `attempts` from `sends`; readouts must use both.
2. **Every reading carries its provenance** — *Base V4 · 6 sessions, 180 days*.
3. **Silence means nothing happened in the log** — not a rest week, not laziness.
4. **Describe the gap, never diagnose the person** — the test being: could this
   sentence be false because of something that happened off-app?
5. **Thin data must read as thin.**
6. **The athlete owns the context the app is missing** — let them say "returning from
   injury" and carry it *next to* the figure. No modelling; visibility is the cheap 90%.

### The satisfying part

Three of these are already followed by accident. `lib/coach.js` labels a stale grade
*"(all time — nothing consistent in the last 90 days)"* so the LLM cannot build
confident advice on it. The Shameometer's **today is never missed** and
pre-`remindFrom` rules are the same instinct about not scoring the unscoreable. The
document mostly writes down an instinct the project already had, so it is followed on
purpose rather than by luck.

It also names the weakest surface: **friends / public profile publishes a bare grade
with no basis at all.**

No code changed. Docs only.

- **`Dashboard.jsx`** — `readPyramid()` at module scope returns the readiness plus the

### Rows inverted

Easiest grade at the top, target at the bottom — a funnel narrowing to the goal. Ben
asked for it, and it turned out to be an internal-consistency fix too: `GradeChart`,
*on the same widget*, has always sorted easiest-first, so the old hardest-first pyramid
ran the ladder the opposite way to the chart six pixels below it.

### The defect the real data found

This is the one worth writing down. The rope pyramid over 180 days builds:

```
6b+ 1/1 MET · 6b 2/2 MET · 6a+ 4/4 MET · 6a 7/8 short 1
```

Three rows full, the base **one send short** — and it scored **1, "No base yet"**. The
same mark as a climber who has never tied in.

Cause: readiness counted *consecutive met tiers from the bottom*, so a single missing
send zeroed everything above it. Counting whole tiers can only ever answer in quarters.
And bottom-up counting was itself written the day before, to fix the opposite defect
(a full easy row carrying an empty top read 57% ready for V6).

Both were attempts to get a structural answer out of a tier **count**. The fix is to
stop counting tiers and measure the thinnest one:

```
base  = tiers below the target     // the target tier is not part of its own base
pct   = min(have / need) over base
score = complete ? 5 : clamp(1..4, round(1 + pct * 4))
```

Excluding the target tier matters independently: an empty top row is the *normal* state
of a goal — not having done it yet is why it is a goal — so counting it dragged every
unstarted goal to the floor. What the target holds is a different fact, and
`sentTarget` / `describeTargetEvidence` already carried it.

The old defect stays fixed: an empty V5 row is a zero-width link and holds a V6 reading
at 1 however much V3 sits underneath. It now discriminates *harder* than before — a V4
climber with a broad base reads `Base complete` for V5 and a flat **0%** for V6, where
the old model gave a comfortable-looking 50%.

`complete` now means the *base* is complete; the label only says "Pyramid complete"
when the target has been sent too. `nextUp` names the weakest tier rather than the
biggest absolute shortfall, so the sentence and the mark talk about the same row.

## The goal picker, run with

Ben: *"I like the goal picker thingy. Run with it."*

The picker was eighteen identical grey chips, V0 to V17. The reading that tells you
which of them your log supports already existed — it just wasn't on the thing you were
choosing with, so the only way to find out that your base is complete for 6b and empty
for 6c+ was to tap each chip in turn and read the pyramid that appeared underneath.

### `pyramidLadder()`

New in `lib/pyramid.js`. Scores **every** grade off one pass over the log — the pyramid
is built once and each rung reads it, so eighteen grades cost one walk, not eighteen.
Returns the rungs plus `ready`: the hardest grade whose base is complete.

Named `ready`, not `suggested` or `recommended`, and the distinction is the point. It
says *the log fully supports an attempt at this grade*. It is not advice about what to
climb, and a grade with no base is not a grade the climber cannot do — only one the log
says nothing about.

### `GradeTargetPicker`

The design question that took the longest: the obvious move is to tint every chip by
`SCORE_COLOR`, which is what the rest of the app uses. **That paints twelve of eighteen
chips red**, because almost every grade on the ladder is above anyone's base. A wall of
red on a picker reads as a refusal to let you choose — wrong on the facts, and the exact
inversion of the data-honesty rule that absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.

So the ramp is **one-sided**: grades the log supports get marked, grades it says nothing
about stay plain and stay fully selectable. Nothing gates Save.

Two bands, not three. The first cut had separate tints for "base complete" and "base
nearly there", which needed a three-entry legend to be readable — and the extra shade
was distinguishing something the chip's tooltip and the pyramid below it both already
say in words. Two tints, two legend entries, one line.

### Ben's rope ladder

```
[4][5][5+][6a][6a+][6b•]   green   — base complete
[6b+][6c]                  amber   — base part-built
[6c+] … [9c]               plain   — nothing logged
                           • 6b = hardest base you have
```

The whole story in one row: owns up to 6b, working on 6b+ and 6c, nothing above. His
boulder ladder is entirely plain with no legend at all, which is correct — one session
in 180 days has nothing to say, and a legend for markings nobody can see is noise.

The sheet now builds the ladder once per discipline (memoised on `type` and `sessions`,
not on the selected grade), and the live pyramid underneath reads that same prebuilt
pyramid instead of walking the log again on every tap.

### Auto-achieve is the load-bearing decision

Base reads lower, and auto-achieve compares the current reading against the target by
ladder index. Pointed at the base, a goal would not tick off until **8 credited sends at
the target** — which is the *become a 7a climber* rule, applied to goals that were every
one of them created meaning *send a 7a*. Q1 would have quietly answered Q2.

So the old reading survives under its own name, `getAchievementValueDetail`, with its
90-day window and its all-time guard, and the six tests that covered it followed it
there rather than being deleted. They are now what stops the two collapsing back
together.

### What it reads on a real-shaped log

Plan › Goals and the Dashboard are both behind sign-in, so the running app could not be
driven. Ran the reader over a rope log built to the shape recorded from Ben's export

**One send short of a base is the difference between a grade and no grade at all**,
which is the sharp edge of choosing Base and worth watching on a real log. The blunter
consequence: `calcGoalProgress` reads `null` as zero, so **the progress bar sits at 0%
until a base exists**. The pyramid and its sentences still render beneath it, so the
card is not empty. If that is too blunt, the fix is to let the bar fall back to
readiness `pct` while the number stays absent — a bar and a grade are different claims.
