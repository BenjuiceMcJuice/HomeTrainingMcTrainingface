### The diagnosis

Yesterday's three defects were not three mistakes. They were **one abstraction
failing three times.** `calcConsistentGrade` crushes a whole log into a single
grade, and every fix since has been an attempt to smuggle a discarded piece back
in — four tuning constants in a day, each existing so that one number could not
lie in one particular way.

The log, meanwhile, is already pyramid-shaped: one row per climb with a grade, an
outcome and a date. `GradeChart` even draws it. Nothing reads it.

### The research, and what it will not support

Checked rather than assumed, since the design rests on it.

- **The pyramid is standard and old.** Hörst, *How to Climb 5.12*; now common
  coaching material. Canonical shape **1 · 2 · 4 · 8**, 2:1 per tier, base at a
  grade you send consistently.
- **Progression does slow sharply.** Beginner V0 → V2 in months; V4–V6 roughly
  6–12 months a grade; past V8, years. `LEVEL_FACTOR` points the right way.
- **Not claimed:** the widely-copied "slightly more than a tripling in difficulty
  per V-grade". That is a blog summary, not a measurement, and no arithmetic
  rests on it. The literature justifies a *shape*, not a constant.
- **The caveat:** Power Company's point that lower tiers are about *variety*, not
  count. BetaLog cannot see variety, so every figure counts sends, not distinct
  problems, and says so.

### Two things found in the data model

- **`Climb.attempts` is dead** — typed as a number, hardcoded to `1` at the only
  call site. One tap is one climb; counts come from rows.
- **`Climb.routeId` is always null**, so six sends of the same V4 are
  indistinguishable from six different ones. A pyramid is explicitly about
  distinct climbs. This is the one real gap, and it shaped the design.

### Phase 1 — `lib/pyramid.js`, wired to nothing

Three readings replace one number: **Project** (hardest sent, ≥1), **Working**
(hardest genuinely tried, ≥3 attempts — deliberately the same 3
`calcConsistentGrade` already uses), **Base** (hardest grade whose own pyramid is
complete, defined by reusing `pyramidReadiness` rather than a fresh threshold).

Three rules do the work: **surplus spills downward** (ten 6c+ sends demonstrate
the 6c tier; one 7a send fills only the top tier), **one session cannot fill a
tier** (`MAX_SENDS_PER_SESSION`, the cheapest honest defence against laps given no
route identity), and **no minimum-evidence guard is needed** — a thin log makes
empty tiers, which is the truth.

Readiness is `credited / required`, and `score = 1 + pct × 4`. **The scale is the
completeness, so there is nothing to tune.**

#### The bug phase 1 found in itself

A V2 pyramid has only V1 and V0 beneath it, so the four-tier shape cannot be
built. Unhandled, V2 "completed" on three tiers and `baseGrade` returned **V2 for
a climber working V4** — a lower bar at the bottom of the ladder, making the
reading incomparable. Now `truncated: true`, never `complete`; beginners have no
Base and callers show Working or Project. Found by a failing test whose
expectation was also wrong, which took some care to separate.

#### What it says on a realistic log

```
TARGET 6b+   Pyramid complete   (5/5, 100% built)
TARGET 6c    Base nearly there  (4/5,  87% built)   next up: 6b+ (1 more)
TARGET 7a    No base yet        (1/5,   7% built)   next up: 6b+ (7 more)
```

Three goals, three honest and actionable answers, one model, no special cases.
Against yesterday's "Unlikely as set" plus four constants, this is the argument
for the rewrite.

### The open question phase 1 exists to answer

**Is the four-tier shape too demanding for real logs?** A textbook 2/4/8 at
V4/V3/V2 still fails "own V4" at 14/15, because the shape wants eight sends at V1
and nobody logs warm-ups. Either the shape is right and most people's Base is
genuinely lower than they think — which is what the coaching literature actually
claims — or `PYRAMID_SHAPE` wants softening for logged-data reality. Wants
checking against Ben's real log before phase 2 touches a screen.

### Decisions

Ben approved the spec and phase 1 without ruling on the four decisions
individually. Each ships as a **named, changeable parameter** carrying my
recommendation — `MAX_SENDS_PER_SESSION`, `PYRAMID_WINDOW_DAYS`, ladder-rung
tiers — and none is baked in. Friends comparison untouched.

## Phase 1, revised — Ben on pyramid depth

> I think you're right that V2 and below probably don't need to factor that much as
> most people can do a V2 first couple of tries and these low level ones won't get
> logged. Also. The harder you climb this is likely to happen for V3s also. Almost
> like the pyramid can only ever be x rows in depth based on what your current
> 'grade' is if that makes sense?

It makes sense, it is right, and it turned out to be two separate findings once the
numbers were run. Phase 1 exists to be wrong in private, and it was — twice.

### 1. Depth capped at three tiers

Measured against a log whose hardest send was **V4**:

| Depth | "ready for V6" | "ready for V7" |
|---|---|---|
| 4 tiers | 80% | **53%** |
| 3 tiers | 33% | **0%** |

53% ready for V7 having never sent a V5 is nonsense. The fourth tier sits in warm-up
territory, so it is *simultaneously* never logged and trivially filled by volume that
means nothing — harsh on honest logs and far too generous on ambitious goals at the
same time. `PYRAMID_MAX_DEPTH = 3`.

**This reverses yesterday's truncation rule.** I had made a short pyramid at the
bottom of the ladder disqualifying, to stop `base` dropping to V2 for a V4 climber.
Under Ben's framing that was treating the symptom: a shallow pyramid at a low grade is
the *correct* shape. `truncated` is still reported, no longer disqualifying.

### 2. Readiness is built from the bottom, stopping at the first gap

Capping the depth exposed the next layer. The same V4 climber still read **57% ready
for V6**, because the V4 row was full while V5 and V6 were empty — total-material
counting let the easy tiers carry an absent top.

A pyramid with a hole in it is not partly built; it is built *up to the hole*. That is
true of real pyramids and it is what makes the figure discriminate:
`pct = solidTiers / depth`, counting consecutive met tiers upward from the base. Total
material survives as `fillPct`, and `topTierMet` / `sentTarget` stay separate so a
send at the target is never hidden.

### 3. `baseGrade` was answering the wrong question

Found while checking the above. It was "the hardest grade whose pyramid is complete" —
but readiness asks *can I get there*, and a single send on a broad base answers yes.
On a well-logged V4/V5 season with one V6 in it, **Base reported V6**.

Ownership is a different question: have you done it repeatedly? Base now reads the
spread — hardest grade with `credited ≥ max(shape)` — and answers **V4** on that log.
Twenty-four V4 sends against four V5s and one V6 is what a coach would say.

### Where it landed

```
SOLID — a V4/V5 season with one V6 in it
  project V6 · working V5 · base V4
  V5: 100%  Pyramid complete    V5 1/1   V4 2/2   V3 4/4
  V6: 100%  Pyramid complete    V6 1/1   V5 2/2   V4 4/4
  V7:  33%  Base thin           V7 0/1*  V6 1/2*  V5 4/4

SPARSE — hardest send V4
  project V4 · working V4 · base V4
  V5:  67%  Base nearly there   V5 0/1*  V4 2/2   V3 4/4
  V6:  33%  Base thin           V6 0/1*  V5 0/2*  V4 4/4
  V7:   0%  No base yet         V7 0/1*  V6 0/2*  V5 0/4*
```

Six answers, one model, no special cases. The sparse climber is told plainly that V5
is nearly there and V7 is not a conversation yet.

### Still open

- Should depth **vary** with the target's height rather than being flat three? Ben's
  phrasing allows for it and it is plausible — there is more meaningful room below V10
  than below V3. Flat three is what the evidence supports; a curve would be invented
  numbers.
- Does this describe a **real** log better than the number does? Still the question
  phase 2 waits on, and it wants Ben's actual export rather than fixtures.
