## BTL-B10 — a gym session scores by what was in it

A flat 2 made ten minutes of ankle alphabet count the same as an hour of lifting, and it showed:
backfilling the log scored **w/c 13 April at 100, EXCELLENT**, off five consecutive days of ankle
rehab physio. `difficulty` could not fix it — those were logged as 2 like everything else — so the
score reads exercise count, the one thing that actually separates rehab from training.

Bands, not a per-exercise rate: the difference worth capturing is rehab vs training vs a full
session, not one extra accessory lift. **The middle band is the old flat value**, so an ordinary gym
session is unchanged and only the extremes move. That week goes 10 training points → 5, against a
target of 9.

**An existing test caught the first version.** It asserted a bare `{type:'gym'}` scores 2; my first
pass scored it 1. That would have inferred "rehab" from an empty exercise list — absence of evidence
treated as evidence of a small session. A gym log with no exercises is missing detail, so it keeps
the old value. `SCORE_VERSION` 1 → 2; sealed weeks keep version 1 and are never rescored.

## BTL-B14 — which build is this device running?

Three facts, because no one of them answers the question. **Build** is the commit Cloudflare built
from and changes every deploy. **Cache** is read from the *running* service worker rather than from
source — a device serving a stale bundle has the stale worker too, so asking the worker is the
difference between what should be installed and what is. **Version** is last, because
`package.json` only changes when somebody bumps it.

`CF_PAGES_COMMIT_SHA` has no `VITE_` prefix so Vite will not expose it on its own; injected via
`define`. Verified end to end by building with the variable set and grepping the bundle for it.
eslint gains two *scoped* blocks — node globals for `vite.config.js`, the injected constants for
`buildInfo.js` — rather than loosening globals everywhere.

## The 6c forecast — a diluted rate, and a date with the wrong name

Ben, on his rope card right after that release: *"does the base look at last 180, 90 or 30 days? It
still doesn't feel right somehow."*

Three windows on one card: 90 days for the header and the red dot line, 180 for the pyramid and the
rate, and the 90d toggle on the bars. Working the numbers back from his screenshot (6c target,
shortfall 4, 10 credited sends, conversion 60 days) gave exactly the date shown:

```
fill        4 / (10 / 180 days)  = 72 days
conversion  French default 60 x Advanced 1.0 = 60 days
total       132 days             -> late January
```

**Two faults in that.**

- **The rate was diluted.** It divided by all 180 days, including the months before his first climb
  in the window. It now runs from the first climb to today, never under 28 days and never over the
  window. `buildPyramid` returns `firstDate` for it.
- **The words misnamed the date.** *"Base built around late January"* — but that date is the base
  **plus** the time to move up a grade. The base itself was due in November. The headline now reads
  *"Ready for 6c around …"*, and a new line splits it: *"Base full in about N weeks, then about 9
  weeks to move up a grade (a typical time — your log has no grade change to measure yet)."*

With the rate fixed, the 60-day conversion is now most of the date. It is the stated default, not
measured, and the new line says so, which is the next thing for Ben to judge.

## BTL-B6 — two kinds of grade goal, send by default

Q2 answered. Ben: *"I think I was most happy when I did my first 7a, not when I became more
consistent. However it's a good distinction."*

- **`Goal.kind`** is `send` or `become`, chosen in the goal sheet as *Send a 6c* / *Become a 6c
  climber*. A goal with no kind reads as `send`, which is what every existing goal was created
  meaning — so **no migration**, and no goal changes meaning on deploy.
- **`goalMet`** in `lib/goals.js` is now what auto-achieve ticks off by. *Send*: one send or flash at
  the grade or harder, in the goal's disciplines, dated on or after the day the goal was set.
  *Become*: the base grade reaches the target.
- **The old reading is deleted.** Auto-achieve used a 90-day consistent grade with an all-time guard
  — neither kind, held only until this question was answered. That closes **BTL-B18** too: the
  second window it existed to reconcile is gone.
- The kind shows on the goal card title, the achieved row, and the Dashboard goal line (*→ send 6c*).

**Nothing that ticked off before can fail to now.** A 90-day consistent grade at the target implies
sends at it, so *send* is strictly the easier rule for existing goals. Sends from before a goal was
set do not count, so an old season cannot tick a new goal on deploy.

**Left open — BTL-B33.** A *become* goal's forecast still projects readiness to *send* the target,
not to own it. Filed rather than half-built.
